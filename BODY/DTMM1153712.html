FAQs and their solutions

Reminder

This document summarizes the common problems and solutions that may be encountered during spraying.

Fish eyes

  • Cause:

    • Dirty cloth is used for degreasing, and the vehicle is not completely degreased.

    • The compressed air contains water or oil.

    • The spray booth is contaminated by chemical substances, such as common surface panel wax for sheet metal painting, bolt loosening agent, vehicle body paint maintenance wax, and other materials.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Thoroughly degrease the vehicle before spraying. When degreaser is used, use two pieces of cloth for wiping in turn.

    • Regularly check the oil-water separator of the air compressor, and drain water in time if necessary.

    • Do not use silicon-containing products in the painting workshop or spray booth.

    • Select water-soluble polishing wax without silicon wax for polishing.

  • Solution: After grinding the coating with fish eyes, add anti-fish eye solution to the spraying material and re-spray. Refer to

    • Solvent-based plain paint 

    • Water-based metallic paint

Dust spots

  • Cause:

    • Dust particles remain on the vehicle surface and paint is contaminated during spraying.

    • Dust adheres to the paint surface during walking.

    • The filter cotton of spray booth is blocked, there is dust on the floor, the walls are dirty, and the top surface needs to be replaced.

    • The material is not filtered before spraying.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Clean up all openings around the repair area.

    • Mask the area required to be masked with the masking paper by folding its tear edge inward and facing its smooth side outward.

    • Filter the paint before it is poured into the spray gun.

    • During operation, wear clean, fiber-free and anti-static workwear (nylon).

    • Treat plastic surfaces with anti-static agent before spraying.

      Caution

      Degreasing is not required after the plastic surface is treated with anti-static agent.

    • Regularly maintain the spray booth and replace the filter materials in it.

    • Wipe the air pipe before spraying.

  • Solution: After grinding the coating with fish eyes, add anti-fish eye solution to the spraying material and re-spray. Refer to

    • During spraying, the needle tip can be used to remove dust in the paint film.

    • Small dust in the dry coating can be removed by polishing. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If dust is trapped deep in the coating, the surface should be ground and repainted. Refer to:

      • Water-based plain paint

Orange peel effect (orange peel)

  • Cause:

    • Too high spraying viscosity

    • Too high drying speed of thinner

    • Too high or too low spraying pressure, and too large nozzle

    • Too high or too low ambient temperature

    • Excessive anti-fish eye solution added to the paint to be used

  • Preventive measures:

    • Mix in the correct proportions with a scale and check viscosity.

    • According to the ambient temperature, workpiece size and airflow rate, select the thinner with reference to the technical information manual.

    • Spray at a temperature of about 20°C.

    • Properly use the anti-fish eye solution to ensure a correct mixing ratio.

  • Solution:

    • Slight orange peel effect can be removed by polishing. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If it is serious, the surface must be ground and repainted. Refer to:

      • Water-based plain paint 

      • Solvent-based plain paint 

      • Water-based metallic paint 

      • Solvent-based metallic paint 

      • Spraying of plastic parts 

Sagging

  • Cause:

    • The substrate is not thoroughly degreased so that the paint cannot adhere to the substrate firmly.

    • The drying speed of the selected thinner is too slow, and too much thinner is added to the paint.

    • Paint builds up in some areas due to too short spraying distance or uneven spraying.

    • The ambient temperature is too low and the thinner volatilizes too slowly.

  • Preventive measures:

    • According to the workpiece size, ambient temperature and airflow rate, select the thinner with reference to the technical information manual.

    • Before spraying, the sprayed surface shall be degreased by reasonable methods.

    • Spray at a temperature of about 20°C.

  • Solution:

    • Slight sagging can be treated by grinding and polishing. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If it is serious, the surface must be ground and repainted. Refer to:

      • Water-based plain paint 

      • Solvent-based plain paint 

      • Water-based metallic paint

Whitening and fogging

  • Cause:

    • The quick-drying thinner is used to sharply cool the sprayed surface, resulting in immediate condensation of water vapor on the wet coating surface.

    • Excessive spraying pressure causes the workpiece to cool down, followed by moisture condensation.

    • The sprayed panel is not completely dried before being put into use, and condensate invades the coating.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Use slow-drying thinner in wet weather.

    • Avoid spraying in a wet and cold environment.

    • Regularly check the airflow rate of spray booth.

    • Select the corresponding drying time according to the workpiece temperature.

  • Solution:

    • If the problem is not serious, just polish the repaired area. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If it is serious, the surface must be ground and repainted. Refer to:

      • Water-based plain paint 

      • Solvent-based plain paint 

      • Water-based metallic paint 

      • Solvent-based metallic paint 

      • Spraying of plastic parts 

Spotting (clouding)

Clouding only occurs in metallic primer. Uneven aluminum pigment in the primer causes uneven color, like clouding or spotting.

  • Cause:

    • Quick-drying thinner is used.

    • Incorrect spray gun setting and incorrect spraying air pressure and distance cause too heavy primer.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Select the thinner based on ambient temperature, repair area and airflow rate.

    • Use the recommended settings for spray guns.

    • Spray primer according to the technical data guide.

  • Solution:

    • After the varnish is sprayed, sand it with dry sandpaper P500 or wet sandpaper P1000, and then respray according to the precautions mentioned above.

    • When primer spraying is still under way, respray it according to the recommended procedure or spraying technique.

Pinhole

  • Cause:

    • Poor quality of the selected thinner

    • Incorrect spraying pressure or improper nozzle size

    • Too thick coating and too short interval between coatings

    • No volatilization time given

    • Manual drying immediately after spraying, and too high drying temperature

    • Infrared drying equipment used too early, or too short distance from equipment to vehicle

  • Preventive measures:

    • Select high-quality thinner suitable for ambient temperature.

    • Select the appropriate spray pressure and nozzle size.

    • Use the correct spraying technique and give sufficient volatilization time.

    • Follow the recommended volatilization time to allow the last layer of paint to volatilize for a period before manual drying.

    • Allow the coating to volatilize normally before infrared drying, and keep a proper distance between the infrared equipment and the vehicle.

  • Solution: Grind the paint film surface until all solvent bubble marks are removed, and then respray it.

Wrinkling and paint lifting

  • Cause:

    • The selected coating does not match the substrate.

    • The adhesion between the previous coating and substrate is poor.

    • It is repainted before the substrate is completely dry or hardened.

    • The paint is sprayed too much at one time.

    • The volatilization time is too long, and the previous volatile coating is dissolved by the solvent of the next layer of paint.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Analyze the substrate of the repair area and select a suitable primer for the substrate, adopt the correct mixing ratio and use appropriate spraying method to prevent excessive thickness.

    • Apply the next coating after a suitable volatilization time.

  • Solution:

    • To some extent, after the lifted paint is completely dry and then ground to a sound paint layer, perform repainting.

    • For more sensitive substrates, paint carefully: the coating should be thin, and sufficient volatilization time is required.

    • In case of serious paint lifting, it must be completely removed before repainting. Refer to:

Gloss loss

  • Cause:

    • The wet coating absorbs wax or other similar contaminants.

    • The poly-putty is not completely dry before grinding.

    • The grinding sandpaper is too coarse, and the drying speed of selected thinner is too high.

    • The drying agent does not match the paint.

    • Improper amount of drying agent or thinner is added.

    • The mixing method of each component is incorrect (two-component product).

    • The baking temperature is too high or the baking time is too long.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Thoroughly degrease the repair area and its surroundings before grinding and spraying.

    • Set the drying time according to ambient temperature and paint film thickness.

    • Select and use appropriate sandpaper.

    • Select the drying agent recommended by the paint manufacturer and compatible with the thinner.

    • Mix the paint with the help of a scale bar to ensure a correct mixing ratio.

    • Follow the recommended volatilization time and prevent excessive spraying from causing excessive coating thickness.

    • Select the appropriate drying time before polishing according to the instructions in the product technical manual.

  • Solution:

    • Polish to increase glossiness. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If it cannot be repaired, grind it before repainting. Refer to

      • Solvent-based plain paint 

      • Water-based metallic paint

Sandpaper marks

  • Cause:

    • The selected sandpaper is too coarse, leaving coarse sandpaper marks after grinding the repair area.

    • The primer or poly-putty coating is not completely dry before grinding.

    • Scratches are caused by sand particles or coarse dust particles during grinding.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Use sandpaper with 100 meshes finer than that in the repair area for grinding.

    • Make sure that the substrate coating is sufficiently dry before grinding.

    • Remove all dust by dust removal or blow gun before grinding.

    • Use sandpaper of one grade finer for manual grinding.

  • Solution:

    • After the paint film is dry, grind the top coat with appropriate sandpaper and then polish the area. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If it is too serious, grind it before repainting. Refer to:

      • Water-based plain paint 

      • Solvent-based plain paint

Water marks

  • Cause:

    • Improper amount of drying agent is added.

    • The coating is thick and it cannot be completely dried within the recommended time.

    • The coating fails to dry properly.

    • The coating contacts with rainwater or water droplets during its cooling.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Mix in the correct proportions with the aid of a scale.

    • Avoid excessive thickness.

    • Dry the coating according to the standard drying time and temperature.

    • Use sandpaper of one grade finer for manual grinding.

    • Let the vehicle cool down before it contacts with liquid.

  • Solution:

    • Polish the surface until marks disappear. Refer to Performing polishing 

    • If it is too serious, grind it before repainting. Refer to:

Poor hardening or poor drying of coating

  • Cause:

    • Incorrect hardener used, hardener expired or reacted with moisture in the air.

    • Too low drying temperature, too short drying time or insufficient air flow in the spray booth resulting in insufficient drying of coating.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Use a hardener suitable for the ambient temperature.

    • Mix according to the technical data description.

    • Check the service life and sealing reliability of hardener before use.

    • Store the paint product at a suitable temperature.

    • Check the filter and maintain the spray booth regularly.

  • Solution:

    • Dry the panel for a long time at the temperature stated in the product manual. Refer to How to dry 

    • If the wrong product is used, remove the paint with thinner or by grinding and repaint as required. Refer to

Poor adhesion

  • Cause:

    • Mismatched products are used.

    • Incorrect and poor-quality degreasing product is used. The release agent on new bumper is not removed (new component).

    • Incorrect grade and material of sandpaper or insufficient grinding is used.

    • The mismatching plastic primer is used, resulting in poor adhesion and thus unqualified paint coating.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Use OEM certified supporting products.

    • Thoroughly clean the panel surface with recommended degreaser and two pieces of wiping cloth.

    • Grind with sandpaper of an appropriate grade.

    • Select a suitable primer according to the type of substrate.

  • Solution: Remove the entire paint system until it is smooth. If necessary, remove the entire paint system and prepare and paint as recommended. Refer to

Blistering

  • Cause:

    • Dirt remains on the substrate before spraying.

    • Water vapor condenses on the vehicle after degreasing.

    • Calcium and salt deposits in the grinding water are still left on the vehicle and absorb moisture.

    • The polyester poly-putty has been treated by water grinding or absorbed the moisture in the air.

    • Inappropriate drying agent or thinner is used.

    • The drying agent has reacted with water vapor in the tank.

    • The compressed air contains oil or water.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Check the substrate carefully before and during degreasing.

    • Dry grind the polyester poly-putty.

    • Cover the drying agent tank and seal it immediately after use.

    • Regularly check the oil-water separator of air compressor and drain water regularly as required.

  • Solution: Remove the defective coating and repaint as required. Refer to:

    • Water-based plain paint 

    • Solvent-based plain paint 

    • Water-based metallic paint

Falling off

  • Cause:

    • The panel substrate is not sufficiently ground.

    • The degreasing is insufficient or degreaser of poor quality is used.

    • Thinners that dry too fast were selected.

    • The mismatching plastic primer is used, resulting in poor adhesion and thus unqualified paint coating.

    • The primer is too thin and rough, and no tack cloth is used between the paint layers and in the last layer.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Improve the grinding quality before spraying top coat.

    • Use only suitable, quality-assured and correct degreaser.

    • Select the thinner based on ambient temperature, repair area and airflow rate.

    • Select a suitable primer according to the type of substrate.

  • Solution: Remove the paint system to the metal surface and grind out feather edges. If necessary, apply poly-putty and repaint. Refer to

Poly-putty marks

  • Cause:

    • The substrate is not completely hardened so that it absorbs the top coat.

    • The sandpaper is too coarse, and improper thinner is used for the top coat.

    • The parts have not been properly primed and sealed, and repaired with poly-putty or filler putty before spraying the top coat.

  • Preventive measures:

    • Grind the repaired part until the metal layer is exposed.

    • At the edge of exposed paint layer, use thinner for solvent test. If the paint is softened, this layer must be sealed.

    • Select suitable sandpaper and make sure that all preparatory materials are completely dry.

    • Poly-putty and filler putty can only be used on exposed metal instead of covering the original paint surface.

  • Solution: After the repair material is dry, grind flat the damaged part and then separate it with priming material before repainting. Refer to:

Spots and blurring - metallic paint

  • Cause: The uneven distribution of glitter pigments and the uneven thickness and glossiness of coatings lead to uneven reflection light in various colors (blurring or spotting).

  • Preventive measures:

    • Methods of removing spots: Generally, it is solved by a medium-dry spraying method. In order to prevent uneven aluminum powder, the four parameters of spray gun operation should be maintained, and the coating thickness should be uniform.

    • Timing for removing spots: After the primer is sprayed, the solvent in the paint film begins to volatilize. The brightness of the wet paint film decreases and the matte finish reaches 50%~70% of the original glossiness, then apply metallic paint to remove spots.

    • Varnish layer spots: If the varnish is applied immediately after the aluminum powder paint is applied, it will cause spots. Therefore, a slightly longer interlayer standing time shall be allowed after spraying aluminum powder paint. After confirming that the primer is dry, properly spray the varnish coating and avoid over-wetting.

  • Solution: Repaint. Refer to:

    • Water-based metallic paint 

    • Solvent-based metallic paint 

Darkening of the blend area - metallic paint

  • Phenomenon: When the edge of the blend area of aluminum powder paint dries, it can cause the surface color to darken when viewed obliquely.

  • Preventive measures:

    • During transition, apply a resin blender coating on the surface of the blend area.

    • Resin blender can avoid the problem of thinning coating edges and quick drying of coating during spraying, and make the coating surface smooth while enhancing the adhesion of other primers and preventing color darkening.

  • Solution: Repaint. Refer to:
